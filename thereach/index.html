Pilot briefing · The game

Every match moves the war.

You are a mech pilot with a faction, a rank and a clan that counts on you. What you do tonight is still on the map tomorrow.

The Reach: Unknown is a persistent mech war, fought from the cockpit. Join one of five factions, or fly alone as a Torrath mercenary, and fight for ground on a contested world. Every pilot shares one front line, and it never resets.

The Reach

One world.
Five claims on it.

An Earth-like world, with water, around the nearest star system to our own.

It is the first home humanity has found since Earth failed. The Archive set out for it on a voyage of 32.63 years, and every faction aboard means to claim it. None of them agrees on who has the right.

The Archive

Built to remember.

It was named before it left Earth. Not for what it carried, though it carried everything worth saving: for what it was meant to do. Remember, so the next civilization would know what the last one did wrong.

About twenty thousand people made the crossing in it, awake, and its crew kept it running the whole way. Over The Reach it stays in orbit, neutral ground above the war, and every drop to the surface starts here.

The story so far

How we got here.

Earth was failing, and there was a plan. Probes went out to find somewhere new, and the first to answer came from the nearest star: a habitable world, with water. Earth could not wait for a second answer.

A small crew went ahead, several hundred people on a fast ship, to land first and build. Then, on 19 January 3310, the Archive left Earth with about twenty thousand people and everything worth saving: the slowest ship in the plan, and the last to leave.

Fifteen years into our crossing, the crew landed on The Reach. In 3330 their signal reached us: they were down, and they had built a settlement. We sent a faster ship ahead with supplies and more people, and in 3337 it landed.

For years after that, the settlement's logs came home on schedule. The last one arrived in June 3340. Then the signal stopped.

Now The Archive is still coming. Nobody aboard agrees on what the silence means, and when we reach orbit, we will find out.

    Aboard · Five factions

    Nobody was born into a faction. Every member chose.

    Five answers to why Earth failed. Choose one to read its creed, and the page takes its colour.

    Honor

    Kashiro

    “We failed because we let small things go. Precision is honor.”

    The hardest path: honor that costs something real. Not inherited but chosen, which is what makes the code mean something. They fly as a disciplined team under a strict chain of command, and settle disputes by trial of combat.

    Dominion

    Valdron

    “We failed because we were divided. Unity is survival.”

    They believe humanity needs one strong leader. Not cruel: convinced. Commanders and logistics officers who watched the ship's people split apart and decided the answer was more authority, not less. They advance in overwhelming force and hold what they take.

    Shadow

    Darvyn

    “We failed because we believed what we were told. Information is the only honest currency.”

    Not villains: realists. Intelligence officers and analysts who watched good intentions end in catastrophe, stopped trusting intentions and started trusting data. They would rather win before the battle starts.

    Pack

    Gorvik

    “We failed because we each looked after our own. The pack eats first.”

    Not anarchists: tribalists. Small-unit soldiers and crew who watched every large structure fail and decided the only thing worth trusting is the person beside you. Fiercely loyal inside the pack, suspicious of anything larger.

    Foundation

    Shenvong

    “We failed because we never built anything that could hold. What holds, saves.”

    Engineers, architects and long-term planners. They fight because they have to, to make fighting unnecessary. The others are competing for something; Shenvong are building something, and they measure it by what still stands after they are gone.

    Or swear to none of them. Decline all five and you fly as : a mercenary, and free.
